CLUSTER chord glyphs

An abstract visual language for chord quality. Each glyph encodes a chord’s shape and feel without letters or music-theory math. The main circle’s fill is the triad quality (crescent → half → full → gibbous, like moon phases); dots around it mark extensions at their clock hours (7th at 7 o’clock, 9th at 9 o’clock…).

filled dot = natural ringed dot = flat dot + tick = sharp double ring = double flat radial spoke poking out of the main circle = inversion (tone in bass) smaller circle = no fifth (no5) hollow circle = no third (no3) or suspended

No-fifth and no-third chords

When the third or fifth role is absent, the base circle shrinks (no5) or goes hollow (no3). The “no3” shape is intentionally identical to a sus chord — a chord with a fifth and no third genuinely doesn’t tell you major or minor.

  • C (no3) — fifth only (power chord)
  • C (no5) — third only
  • Cm (no5) — minor no5
  • C (no3, no5)

Inversions — the spoke marker

A short black radial line poking out of the main circle at the bass tone’s clock hour. No spoke anywhere means root position.

  • C / E (1st inv)
  • C / G (2nd inv)
  • Cmaj7 1st inv
  • C7 3rd inv (7 in bass)
  • C7♭9 7th-in-bass
  • C9 9th in bass

Microtonal chords

Round each note to the nearest 12-TET semitone, classify the base shape from the rounded 3rd/5th, then mark residual deviations with the existing natural/flat/sharp/double-flat vocabulary at h=3 and h=5. The base shape stays clean — all microtonal nuance lives in satellite markers. 50-cent ties always round up and use the double-flat marker.

  • Hexany 1,2,5 (augmented, microtonal)
  • Harmonic series 4:5:6 (just major, microtonal)
  • Hexany 1,3,5 (sus, microtonal)
  • 24-EDO mid-tie (double-flat 5th)

Scale constellations

A separate glyph family from chord glyphs — it answers “where does this note sit” (proportional position, cents / period × 360°), not “what would a musician call it” (the named-degree clock used by chord glyphs). Each dot is a note, all joined by a thin outline so each scale gets a distinct, recognizable silhouette (whole-tone = hexagon).

  • Major scale
  • Natural minor scale
  • Whole-tone scale (hexagon)
  • Harmonic minor scale
  • Hexany scale
  • Harmonics scale (4:5:6:7)
